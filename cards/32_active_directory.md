# Active Directory / Internal Network Attack Paths

*Keywords: Active Directory, AD, Kerberoasting, AS-REP roasting, BloodHound, SharpHound, NTLM relay, LLMNR NBT-NS poisoning, responder, Pass-the-Hash, Pass-the-Ticket, DCSync, unconstrained delegation, ADCS ESC, secretsdump, crackmapexec netexec, impacket, golden ticket.*

## What AD testing covers and the discipline for it

Internal Active Directory testing is about finding a path from a low-privileged foothold to Domain Admin (or the specific objective in scope). It's noisy and high-impact, so discipline matters: stay inside the authorized internal scope, avoid anything that disrupts production auth (no account-lockout storms, no mass password spraying beyond the agreed threshold, no DoS on domain controllers), use test/provided accounts where possible, and prefer *enumeration and single-proof* over broad exploitation. The workflow is: (1) get a foothold/creds, (2) map the domain and privilege relationships, (3) identify the shortest escalation path, (4) prove one step of it cleanly.

## AD recon & credential access (controlled)

**Map the terrain** with **BloodHound** (collect via `SharpHound`/`bloodhound-python`) — it graphs users, groups, sessions, ACLs, and shows the shortest path to DA without you touching anything. **Enumerate** with `netexec`/`crackmapexec` (`nxc smb <subnet> -u u -p p`), `ldapdomaindump`, `enum4linux-ng`. **Kerberoasting** — request service tickets for SPN accounts and crack offline (`GetUserSPNs.py -request`, then `hashcat -m 13100`); it's low-impact (no auth attempts against DC beyond a normal TGS request). **AS-REP roasting** — for accounts with pre-auth disabled (`GetNPUsers.py`), crack `-m 18200` offline. **Poisoning/relay** — `Responder` for LLMNR/NBT-NS/mDNS to capture NetNTLMv2 hashes, and `ntlmrelayx` to relay to a host without SMB signing; on an authorized internal test these are standard, but they touch other users' traffic — coordinate and keep it time-boxed. Crack captured hashes offline (never lock accounts online).

## AD privilege escalation & lateral movement

From creds/hashes, move with **impacket**: `secretsdump.py` (dump SAM/LSA/NTDS — DCSync if you have the rights: `secretsdump.py -just-dc-user krbtgt`), `psexec.py`/`wmiexec.py`/`smbexec.py` for execution, `getST.py` for ticket attacks. **Pass-the-Hash / Pass-the-Ticket** reuse captured material without the plaintext (`nxc smb -H <NTLM>`). Escalation primitives BloodHound surfaces: `GenericAll`/`WriteDACL`/`ForceChangePassword` over a user or group, `AddMember` to a privileged group, **unconstrained/constrained delegation**, and **ADCS certificate abuse** (ESC1–ESC8 via `certipy`) which is often the fastest DA path in modern domains. **Prove one escalation step** (e.g. you *can* DCSync, shown by dumping a single non-sensitive account's hash, or you *can* add yourself to a group in a test OU) rather than seizing the whole domain — the reachable path is the finding, and a golden-ticket / full-domain takeover demo should only go as far as the rules of engagement allow.

## AD CVSS and remediation

A reliable low-priv→Domain-Admin path (e.g. Kerberoast a weak SPN → DCSync, or ADCS ESC1): `CVSS:3.1/AV:A/AC:L/PR:L/UI:N/S:C/C:H/I:H/A:H` ≈ 8.8 (AV:A adjacent/internal; scope change to the whole domain) — effectively Critical for the environment. Root causes and remediation: **Kerberoasting** → long random passwords for service accounts (or gMSA), remove unnecessary SPNs; **AS-REP** → require Kerberos pre-auth; **relay/poisoning** → disable LLMNR/NBT-NS, enforce SMB signing and LDAP channel binding/signing; **delegation** → remove unconstrained delegation, use resource-based constrained delegation; **ADCS** → fix vulnerable templates (no `ENROLLEE_SUPPLIES_SUBJECT` with client-auth EKU for low-priv enrollers), enable manager approval; enforce tiered admin (no DA logins to workstations), LAPS for local admin passwords, and monitor for DCSync/abnormal ticket requests.
